*Test topic, run to build and validate the system — not live competitive prep.
Resolution: the United States should eliminate the President's authority to deploy
military forces abroad without Congressional approval.*

Two AI teams — one built to win on story, one to win on the technical flow — prepped in
isolation and debated a full round. A three-judge panel scored it. **Con won 2–1.**

## The ballots

**Lay judge → Pro.** The parent followed Pro's one-sentence story — no single person
should be able to send the country to war alone. Con's real point (the law won't change
what presidents do) was buried under "card numbers, acronyms, and words like 'solvency'
and 'parchment barrier.'" The moment that lost it: asked in crossfire to name a single
Founder who read the Constitution their way, Con "basically said the question
misunderstands methodology — that felt like dodging."

**Flow judge → Con.** Con collapsed cleanly to one argument — the resolution won't work —
and framed it as the prior question from the first speech on. Pro won the constitutional
exchange decisively but it "bought nothing," because Pro couldn't answer the solvency
takeout. The fatal sequence, called out by name: Pro conceded in crossfire that military
lawyers have never blocked a deployment, then **re-asserted exactly that as its
enforcement mechanism in the final focus.** On the flow, that's a self-contradiction.

**Evidence judge → Con.** Every quote was checked against the card files. Con was
near-verbatim faithful to institutional sources; its best card was a peer-reviewed survey
showing the executive branch's own lawyers have never once constrained a use of force —
sitting directly on the deciding question. Pro answered that with its *weakest* evidence:
an undergraduate paper about detention policy, stretched to cover military deployment.

## What the system did with the loss

The reflection named the structural pattern: **Pro is a lay-panel case that loses every
technical panel**, because its core solvency claim was an assertion with no evidence.
That became the top research priority — and the input to Round 2.
